Who Pays Interest?

The True Story of Banking and Usury, A True-History Coloring Book (Coloring Edition)

by D.A. Williams

A true-history coloring book on who pays interest, who was forbidden to charge it, and why. Twenty scenes of lending and interest across history, redrawn with big outlines and large open spaces to color.

What is inside

This Coloring Edition follows lending and interest across 4,000 years in 20 scenes. The captions draw on primary religious and classical texts, along with other numbered sources. The pages include Mesopotamian temple loans and Hammurabi's interest caps; what the Torah, the Mishnah, Church councils, and the Qur'an say about charging interest; Athenian bankers, Aristotle, and Rome's rate limits; the Knights Templar, Renaissance Florence, and double-entry bookkeeping; Calvin's letter, England's 10% law, the Bank of Amsterdam, and London's goldsmith bankers; and modern Islamic banking and today's usury laws.

Each of the 20 single-sided coloring pages has its caption, a "Did you know?" fact, and room for notes. The book also has a Who's Who, a timeline, and a full source list with web links.
